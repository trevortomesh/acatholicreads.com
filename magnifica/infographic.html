Pope Leo XIV · First Encyclical

Magnifica Humanitas

On Safeguarding the Human Person in the Time of Artificial Intelligence
Signed 15 May 2026 — the 135th anniversary of Rerum Novarum · 245 paragraphs · Introduction, five chapters, conclusion · Addressed to all people of good will

🏗️ Babel — domination

Genesis 11: one language, one technology, one direction. Power pursued for its own sake, humanity made uniform — and ending in scattering (§§7, 9–10).

or

🧱 Jerusalem — rebuilding

Nehemiah: every family rebuilds its own stretch of the wall, God at the center, shared responsibility, dignity restored piece by piece (§8).

Technology is never neutral in practice — it takes on the characteristics of those who devise, finance, regulate, and use it (§9). The question is which city we are building.

Chapter by Chapter

Introduction · §§1–16

Babel or Jerusalem

  • The "new things" of our age: digitalization, artificial intelligence, robotics (§§3–4)
  • Technology is not the enemy — but its power is now largely private and transnational (§§4–5)
  • The call: "remain profoundly human" — builders of communion, not architects of Babel (§§15–16)
Chapter One · §§17–45

A Dynamic Approach Faithful to the Gospel

  • Social Doctrine is not a handbook of rules but "a process of shared discernment" (§27)
  • The story from Rerum Novarum (1891) through Vatican II to Pope Francis (§§30–44)
  • Truth is "a good to be shared," not a territory to defend (§25)
Chapter Two · §§46–89

Foundations and Principles of Social Doctrine

  • Dignity is a gift that "precedes and transcends each person" — never earned (§50)
  • The first right is the right to life, from conception to natural end (§55)
  • The principles below are applied to data, platforms, and algorithms (§§59–85)
Chapter Three · §§90–130

Technology and Dominance

  • AI has no body, no experience, no moral conscience; its "learning" is statistical adaptation (§99)
  • Three cautions: ease of results, impression of objectivity, simulated communication (§100)
  • AI is not morally neutral (§104); responsibility must be nameable (§105); AI must be "disarmed" of monopoly control (§110)
  • Against transhumanism: "for a person, an error can be a catalyst for profound change" (§128)
Chapter Four · §§131–181

Truth, Work, Freedom

  • Truth is a common good; an "ecology of communication" against disinformation (§§132–137)
  • Work: the person is "an end, not a means" — profit cannot justify sacrificing jobs wholesale (§152)
  • Freedom: the digital attention economy (§170), data colonialism (§178), the person reduced to data (§180)
Chapter Five · §§182–228

The Culture of Power and the Civilization of Love

  • Against a culture that normalizes war: Paul VI's "civilization of love" (§§186–187)
  • "It is not permissible to entrust lethal or otherwise irreversible decisions to artificial systems" (§198)
  • Five paths to peace: disarm words, justice, the victims' perspective, realism, dialogue (§§213–227)
Conclusion · §§229–245

A Program of Christian Life

  • Four pillars: faith in the Incarnation, love, hope, prayer (§§230–245)
  • "No computational system… can create a heart that gives itself, or a conscience that discerns good from evil" (§233)
  • It ends with Mary's Magnificat (§§243–245)

The Six Principles (Chapter Two)

Human DignityGiven, not earned; infinite and inalienable (§§50–55)
The Common Good"The social expression of the dignity recognized in every person" (§59)
Universal Destination of GoodsNow includes patents, algorithms, platforms, and data (§§66–67)
SubsidiarityIn the digital world, the "highest level" is the big tech platforms (§§68–72)
SolidarityA principle and a virtue — the antidote to both welfarism and selfishness (§§73–75)
Social JusticeConfronts "structures of sin"; migrants are its litmus test (§§77–81)
